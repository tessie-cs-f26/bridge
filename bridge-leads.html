Opening Leads

The opponents are in notrump or a suit, and you’ve decided to lead this suit. Which card do you lead?

Lead conventions
  • Doubleton: the top card. A-K: the A. Against notrump, A-K fourth leads the A or K, and A-K fifth or longer leads the A or 4th best.
  • Three cards: with a two-card sequence (10 or higher), always lead the top of the sequence (the K from KQ5, the J from KJ10). With no sequence against notrump, lead low from three to an honor, counting the 10 (the 8 from A98, the 3 from 1073), and the highest from three small (the 8 from 862). Against a suit, lead the lowest (from 9-8-x, the 9 is also accepted).
  • Sequences (four or more cards): the top of a sequence (KQJ, QJ10, J109) or a broken sequence (KQ10, QJ9, J108, 1097), and the top of an interior sequence (the J from KJ10x, the 10 from K109x or A109x). A 9-8 isn’t high enough to count: from J984, lead 4th best (or the 8 with 3rd and low against a suit). Against a suit contract, also the top of two touching honors (the K from KQ73).
  • Against notrump: lead high only from a sequence or near-sequence. Otherwise lead 4th best from a holding with a high card (A, K, Q or J). From KQ73, lead the 3.
  • Against a suit, “3rd and low” setting: 3rd from even, low from odd.
  • Against a suit, “4th best” setting: 4th best from length.
  • Notrump, no high card (four or more cards): 2nd from weakness (the 7 from 9-7-5-3), and 4th best is also accepted. From 10-9-x-x, lead the 10.
  • Against a suit contract you won’t see the A without the K, since you’d rarely lead away from an ace.
  • Each round is a race of 10 holdings: the most right answers wins, and ties go to the faster time.
